Intercepting urban waste to restore natural habitats, rebuild aquatic biodiversity, and secure access to affordable, nutritious food.

The Current State of Bangkok

Bangkok generates over 4 million tonnes of waste annually. Beyond the methane emitted from landfills, one of the most critical failures of our current system is ecological leakage. Plastic pollution and toxic runoff are actively destroying aquatic ecosystems, threatening local wildlife, and eventually reaching the ocean via the Chao Phraya River.

Ecological Leakage

Thailand is identified globally as a major contributor to marine plastic. Plastic suffocates Bangkok's waterways, directly killing fish populations and destroying the habitats necessary for biodiversity to thrive.

Food Chain Contamination

As plastics break down in the khlongs (canals) and the Chao Phraya, microplastics enter the local fish stocks and water supply, directly threatening Bangkok's access to safe, nutritious food.

Social Gaps

Despite community river-cleaning projects, the sheer volume of waste overwhelms local efforts. We lack physical infrastructure that actively protects these habitats from daily urban runoff.

2050 Predictive Modeling

Mathematical forecasting models predict a severe escalation of Bangkok's waste crisis if we maintain current habits.

Business-as-Usual Trajectory

  • Volume Increase: Municipal solid waste will grow to 39.61 million tons by 2050.
  • Habitat Destruction: Global plastic generation is expected to rise 55% by 2050. Without intervention, Bangkok's remaining aquatic habitats will be entirely displaced by synthetic waste.
  • Emission Surge: Urban waste emissions will reach 19.9 million tons of CO2 equivalent over the next 25 years.

The Mitigation Target

By shifting from passive waste management to active ecological defense, we can stop waste at the source. Preventing plastic from entering waterways allows natural habitats to regenerate and drastically reduces the toxic load on our food chain.

The Doughnut Economic Framework

Our physical solution is engineered specifically to operate within the Doughnut Model, bridging the gap between ecological preservation and human needs.

MAIN FOCUS: Natural Habitats & Biodiversity

To stay within our Ecological Ceiling, we must halt the degradation of Bangkok's waterways. Our proposed models serve as physical barriers against pollution and restorative frameworks, preventing urban waste from leaking into sensitive aquatic and terrestrial habitats.

Social Foundation: Access to Nutritious Food

By transforming organic waste into usable fertilizer and physically removing plastics from the water supply, we protect local agricultural yields and fisheries. Keeping microplastics out of the local food chain is essential to ensuring affordable, unpolluted, and nutritious food for Bangkok's residents.

Solution 1: Decentralized Urban Composting Hubs

To directly address the high volume of organic waste, Team 24 proposes an automated, community-scale composting model that transforms food waste into vital agricultural resources.

What It Does

This system intercepts organic and biodegradable waste before it enters municipal collection. By rapidly decomposing food waste at the community level, it produces high-yield, nutrient-rich compost that can be used for local urban agriculture, directly supporting Bangkok's access to affordable and nutritious food while eliminating methane emissions from rotting landfill waste.

How It Works

Using aerobic decomposition chambers equipped with passive aeration and moisture balancing, the model accelerates the breakdown of organics. It features a sealed, pest-proof entry port and a rotational drum mechanism that users or community caretakers can easily operate to mix the compost and maintain optimal oxygen levels.

⚠️ System Downside & Maintenance Constraints

The primary downside is odor and pest management if the carbon-to-nitrogen ratio is not perfectly maintained by the community. Furthermore, it requires strict behavioral compliance; if non-organic waste (like plastic) contaminates the composting chamber, the resulting fertilizer will be unusable for food crops.

Solution 2: Closed-Loop Recycling & Landfill Diversion

To aggressively tackle Bangkok's landfill dependency and prevent ecological leakage, we propose a localized, closed-loop dry waste sorting and compaction model.

What It Does

This infrastructure physically prevents plastic, glass, and metal from reaching open landfills or waterways. By sorting and compacting dry recyclables at the source, it prepares materials for direct sale to recycling plants. This drastically reduces the total mass sent to landfills and protects local Natural Habitats from plastic suffocation and microplastic leakage.

How It Works

The system utilizes standardized, color-coded drop-off points linked to a localized compaction press. Once materials are deposited, a mechanical press reduces the volume of plastics and aluminum, maximizing storage efficiency until collection. This acts as a hyper-local transfer station, bridging the gap between household waste generation and formal recycling facilities.

⚠️ System Downside & Maintenance Constraints

The major constraint is the initial manufacturing and installation cost of the sorting and compaction units. Additionally, the system relies heavily on users pre-washing their plastics; heavily soiled food containers can contaminate the entire batch, attract pests, and ultimately reduce the economic value of the recycled materials.
